The three structures in the `/keeper` studio (`ruled`, `split`, `bleed`) are enough to
demonstrate that *choosing a structure moves the furniture*. They are nowhere near
enough to build a customer's site from. Each one currently renders a masthead, a hero
and one services band — about 5% of a real site.

## What "robust" has to mean

**Full sites, not one band.** A structure has to hold every page a small business
actually needs — home, about, a service menu, contact/booking, a gallery, and a
policies/legal page — and look deliberate on all of them. Right now a structure is a
hero treatment plus a card arrangement, which falls apart the moment there is a page
with no hero.

**More block types.** Live Spirit Seeds ships three (`contentSection` with six layouts,
`service`, `embed`) and that is already the practical floor. A template library needs at
least: testimonials, FAQ, practitioner/team bios, hours + location, pricing or a rate
card, a gallery, a contact form, and a logo/affiliation strip. Every one has to render
in every structure.

**Content-count resilience.** Each structure must survive 0, 1, 3 and 30 images, a
two-word heading and a forty-word one, one service and twelve. The `bleed` structure in
particular has nothing left without photography — that is honest today because the note
says so, but a template that only works with a professional shoot is not a template.

**Accessibility per structure, not once.** The `bleed` overlay puts text on a
photograph; contrast there depends on the image, which no palette check can catch. Each
structure needs its own answer (a scrim floor, a required overlay treatment) rather than
inheriting one from the studio's colour picker.

## The unresolved naming collision

`/keeper` currently shows **six "layouts"** (The Practice, The Stall, The Ledger, …) and
**three "structures"** (Editorial, Sanctuary, Immersive) with no stated relationship, and
a visitor has no way to tell how they compose. They are genuinely different axes —
a layout is *what the site is for* (which blocks, which pages, which data), a structure
is *how it is arranged* — but the page never says so.

Decide and then say it plainly: either every layout is available in every structure (a
grid, and the honest version), or layouts each ship with a default structure. This has
to be settled before the template library is built, because it determines whether the
work is `layouts × structures` or `layouts + structures`.

## Constraint that must not be lost

Whatever this grows into, **structures stay skeletons and never become a client's
appearance** — see ADR 0004. Both named clients gave permission on that condition. A
richer template library makes this easier to violate, not harder: the more complete a
structure gets, the more tempting it is to finish it with somebody's real palette and
photography and call it a starting design.
